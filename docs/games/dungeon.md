# Dungeon — design document

A reconstruction of the original MIT mainframe Zork — the 616-room-and-object
game Infocom later cut into the Zork I/II/III trilogy — on the Gnusto engine.

This document is the story-and-copy source of truth. **Read [Mechanics
contract](#mechanics-contract) before changing anything.** Unlike the other demo
games, this one exists to be faithful to a specific historical artifact, so the
contract here is unusually strict about the world and unusually loose about
nothing.

Two machine-generated companions sit beside it, both regenerated by
`.context/build_atlas.py`:

- [`dungeon-atlas.md`](dungeon-atlas.md) — every room and object extracted from
  the source, with exits, values, and what the trilogy and `Sources/Zork1/`
  already have.
- [`dungeon-prose-comparison.md`](dungeon-prose-comparison.md) — every
  overlapping description, mainframe against trilogy, bucketed by distance.

## What this game is for

The engine ships five worked examples and they are not interchangeable.
`CloakOfDarkness` is the minimal acceptance benchmark. `Lighthouse` is the
shortest complete read. `Gramarye` proves `GnustoSpellcasting` hosts four casting
paradigms. `Fulminate` proves a clock-driven mystery. `KindlyDeep` proves a
survival-and-companion substrate. `Zork1` is the full reconstruction of a
published game.

**Dungeon is the scale test.** At 196 rooms and 209 objects it is roughly three
times `Zork1`, and it asks a question none of the others do: *does an author still
have a good time in this engine at the size of a real, large game?* Where `Zork1`
proves the engine can host a classic, Dungeon proves the engine does not buckle
when the classic is three times bigger and contains four mechanisms nothing in the
engine anticipated.

It is also the first demo game whose prose is **adapted rather than reproduced**,
which makes it the first one where a writer has real work to do.

## Mechanics contract

The middle column is how the reconstruction currently carries each item. The right
column is what must remain true.

| Mechanic | How it is carried | Must survive rewriting |
|---|---|---|
| **The map is the mainframe's** | 196 rooms and their exit tables, extracted from `dung.355` into [`dungeon-atlas.md`](dungeon-atlas.md). | **The topology is not negotiable.** Every room, every exit, every one-way drop. Where a description names its exits, the description yields to the table — never the reverse. |
| **The puzzles are the mainframe's** | Bank of Zork, Royal Puzzle, balloon, robot, mirror box, Endgame, plus everything Zork I kept. | Puzzle *logic* and *solution order* come from the MDL. Trilogy variants of the same puzzle are reference, not authority. |
| **The voice is Infocom's** | Trilogy prose where it fits the mainframe world; newly written in that register where it does not. | The game reads as Zork. It does not read as the terser MDL, and it does not read as a modern pastiche. |
| Scoring | One ceiling, `maxScore` 691 — the main dungeon's 591 plus the endgame's 100. | The total is checked against the `Scoring` award table at bootstrap. Individual values are the original's and are used as-is. |
| `LIGHT-SHAFT` | 10 points, once, for reaching the Lower Shaft lit. An `awardOnce` register, not a room value. | It is an *event* award. Modelling it as a room `VALUE` would pay it on arrival in the dark. |
| Endgame | Reached after the main dungeon; 31 `RENDGAME` rooms worth 100. | The endgame is **in scope**. It is not an optional epilogue. |
| Treasures | 31 valued objects, each with the original's find and case values. | The count and the values are data, used as-is. |
| Two-maxima divergence | The original reports 591 and 100 separately; we report one 691. | This is a **known, deliberate** divergence. It is the only place the scoring model departs from the source, and it belongs in `FIDELITY.md`. |
| Every printed noun answerable | As every other Gnusto game: a named thing the parser does not know reads as a bug. | Non-negotiable, and much harder at this scale than at `KindlyDeep`'s. |

**Free to change:** individual sentences, within the voice constraint; the
title and tagline; how regions are split across files; which plugins carry which
mechanism.

**Not free to change without revisiting this document:** the map, the puzzles,
the treasure values, `maxScore`, and the decision to include the Endgame.

## The two sources, and which one wins

Two separate permissive grants cover the material, and they are not the same
grant. `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES` is the authority; the short version:

| Source | Grant | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Zork I/II/III ZIL | MIT (Microsoft/Activision, Nov 2025) | **Prose**, reproduced verbatim where it fits |
| MDL 1977 / 1978 (MITDDC) | MIT No Attribution | Prose, where the trilogy has no counterpart |
| MDL 1981-07-22 (Supnik 2003) | **No located grant** | **Structure only** — map, values, puzzle logic |

The policy that follows from this, and from the preference for the trilogy's
voice:

1. Where the trilogy line is **identical or near-identical** to the mainframe's,
   take the trilogy line verbatim. Its typography is cleaner.
2. Where the trilogy line **differs substantially**, check it against the room's
   exit table first. The trilogy often changed the prose because it had changed
   the room. Keep the voice; fix the facts.
3. Where the content is **mainframe-only**, write it fresh in the Infocom
   register.

**This makes Dungeon an adaptation, not a reproduction** — the sharpest
difference from `Sources/Zork1/`, which reproduces verbatim throughout. A
contributor who assumes the `Zork1` rule applies here will get it wrong, so
`FIDELITY.md` must say so at the head of the Dungeon section.

A side effect worth noting: under this policy **nothing reproduces 1981 MDL
text**, so the one gap in the licensing picture stops mattering.

## Scoring

| | |
|---|---:|
| Main dungeon (`SCORE-MAX`) | 591 |
| Endgame (`EG-SCORE-MAX`) | 100 |
| **`maxScore`** | **691** |

591 = 115 room values + 466 object find/case values + the 10-point `LIGHT-SHAFT`
award. 100 = the room values of the 31 `RENDGAME` rooms. Both figures are computed
from the source by `.context/build_atlas.py`, not taken from secondary sources —
**the commonly cited "616 points" appears nowhere in the MDL.**

The original keeps the two maxima separate and reports whichever region the player
is in. We sum them into one ceiling, because Gnusto models one `maxScore` and its
bootstrap check earns its keep that way. See the atlas for the full reasoning.

## Map

The authority is [`dungeon-atlas.md`](dungeon-atlas.md), generated from
`dung.355`. Regions, for the purpose of splitting the work across files:

| Region | Rooms | Already in `Sources/Zork1/`? |
|---|---:|---|
| Above ground, house, cellar | ~20 | mostly |
| Round Room hub, dam, temple, Hades | ~35 | mostly |
| Mirror rooms, Atlantis, coal mine | ~30 | mostly |
| Frigid River, falls, rainbow | ~15 | mostly |
| Maze, Cyclops, Treasure Room | ~20 | mostly |
| Bank of Zork | 9 | no |
| Volcano, balloon, Library, Ruby Room | ~12 | no |
| Alice area — Pool, Tea, Posts, Robot, Dingy Closet | ~10 | no |
| Riddle Room, Grail Room, Crypt, Tomb | ~8 | no |
| Royal Puzzle / mirror box | 18 | no |
| Endgame | 31 | no |

Two independent third-party maps were used to cross-check the extraction. Both are
other people's work and **neither is committed to this repository**; they are
consulted, not vendored.

- **Steven Roy's hand-drawn map**, January 1979, revised to January 1982. All 29
  landmarks spot-checked against it are present in the atlas. It deliberately
  omits the Endgame.
- **"The Map of Dungeon"** (`plover.net/~dave/DungeonMap.pdf`), which does cover
  the Endgame — closing exactly the gap the first map leaves. Every Endgame
  landmark on it resolves to a `RENDGAME` room in the atlas.

Between them the extracted map is corroborated end to end by sources independent
of the source code it came from.

## What is new to this game

Four mechanisms have no precedent anywhere in Gnusto. Each is spiked before its
region is built, and each spike answers one question: *in-game rule, new plugin,
or engine change?*

| Mechanism | The hard part |
|---|---|
| **Royal Puzzle** | The room's geometry mutates. The player pushes sandstone walls through a grid; marble walls do not move. Expected to be an in-game rule: a grid in `@Global` and a custom `push <dir>` verb that rewrites exits. |
| **Bank of Zork** | Non-Euclidean. Which room the curtain of light delivers you to depends on hidden state. Expected to fall out of conditional exits. |
| **Balloon** | A vehicle that rises and falls a volcano shaft on a timer, with a receptacle you feed to keep it aloft. Vehicles exist (`player.vehicle`); the vertical daemon does not. |
| **Robot, and the mirror box** | Commanding an actor (`robot, push button`) and riding a vehicle you steer from inside. Load-bearing for the Endgame. **Sized — see below.** |

### The actor-imperative question, answered

This was the spike expected to hurt, and it is much smaller than feared.

**The grammar already exists.** `StandardParser.parse` splits the line on the
first comma, resolves the prefix against scope, and checks the addressee is a
visible actor (`Sources/Gnusto/Parser/StandardParser.swift:100`). What is missing
is only the *dispatch*: every non-greeting order returns
`ParseError.notTakingOrders`, and that refusal is deliberate, documented in
`ActorsAndVehicles.md`, overridable as `GameText.notTakingOrders` (Fulminate
already re-skins it), and pinned by `AddressingTests`:

- `anOrderIsDeclinedRatherThanObeyed` — the order must not take effect
- `anOrderCostsNoTurn` — it is a parse failure, so the clock does not move

So the work is **extending a deliberate, tested seam**, not inventing a grammar:
let a game mark an actor as order-taking, and route the post-comma tokens through
a normal parse with that actor as the agent. Both pinned tests keep their meaning
for actors that have not opted in.

That is engine work in `Sources/Gnusto/`, and per the program plan it becomes its
own phase before any region depends on it — not something improvised inside
`Sources/Dungeon/` at the Endgame.

The remaining three spikes are unstarted.

## Relationship to `Sources/Zork1/`

`Zork1` is **frozen**. Its pinned seed-32 350-point walkthrough must keep passing
untouched; it is the canary for every change made here.

Dungeon is a separate executable target that **forks** Zork1's region bundles as a
starting point and diverges. The line:

- **Content forks.** Different map, different values, different prose policy.
  A shared content library would need a conditional at every seam.
- **Mechanics are shared as plugins**, as the repo already does with
  `GnustoScoring`, `GnustoActors`, `GnustoMeleeCombat`, `GnustoDangerousDark`.
  Anything mainframe-only that is a *mechanism* rather than a *room* becomes a
  plugin or an engine feature — never copied code.

The atlas reports 104 of the 196 rooms already built in `Zork1`, which is a floor:
name matching cannot pair the mazes, so the true overlap is higher.

## Content scope

In scope: the whole 1981-07-22 game, all 196 rooms, all 209 objects, all 691
points, Endgame included.

Out of scope: the 1977 and 1978 variants, except as licensed prose sources; the
Confusion interpreter; any attempt to be byte-compatible with an original
transcript.

## Open questions

1. **The robot/mirror-box parser question.** Sized by its spike. If it needs an
   actor-addressing grammar in `Sources/Gnusto/`, that becomes its own phase
   before any region depends on it.
2. **Maze pairing.** 70 rooms and 142 objects have display names too ambiguous to
   match against the trilogy (fifteen rooms named *Maze*, nine *Dead End*). Pairing
   them needs the exit graph rather than the name, and the atlas does not do it yet.
3. **Objects declared but never placed.** Several valued objects sit in no room's
   contents because they start inside another object or are made by a puzzle. The
   atlas does not yet distinguish those from genuinely unreachable content.
